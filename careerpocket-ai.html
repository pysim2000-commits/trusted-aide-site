Don't let good jobs go to waste

Job searching is brutal. Here's why.

Tailoring takes forever.

Properly tailoring a cover letter and resume takes 30–60 minutes per application. Even if you use ChatGPT to help, the time to coordinate prompts across different tasks is still significant. For a typical job search of 50+ applications, that's 25–50 hours of manual tailoring work — the equivalent of a full working week spent on formatting, not interviewing.

AI is now screening you out before a human even sees your resume.

Competitive roles receive 250+ applications on average. Employers use ATS (Applicant Tracking Systems) to filter candidates automatically — studies suggest up to 75% of resumes are rejected by ATS before a recruiter ever reads them. Beyond format and keyword screening, AI is increasingly being applied to interpret and evaluate your CV content directly. Without AI on your side, your qualifications may never reach a human decision-maker.

Nothing remembers what you sent.

You tailor a resume, hit send, and move on. Three months later, an interview invite arrives — and you can't remember how you positioned yourself or what version you sent. Not remembering the tailored content you submitted risks the hard-earned interview opportunity. The average job search takes 3–6 months. Interview calls regularly come long after the application was filed.

Your interview insights evaporate.

With multiple applications in flight at once, every screening call and interview produces signals — what the interviewer cares about, culture clues, salary norms, gaps to address. Without a place to capture them per application, they're lost.

Each application repeats the same cycle — instead of getting smarter.

What if your job search could compound? What if every application you sent, every note you took, and everything you learned made the next one sharper — with an advisor that remembers all of it?
